The health module joins resident-weighted MSOA delivery exposure to modelled QOF prevalence for obesity, diabetes, hypertension and stroke/TIA. Models adjust for demographic composition, non-health deprivation, population density, rurality and region.

The preliminary estimates do not support a single universal relationship between “fast-food exposure” and health. Results depend on the outcome and whether exposure is expressed as count, population-standardised density or market share.

That instability is part of the finding. Delivery availability is not ordering, consumption, diet or individual exposure, and spatial modelling cannot resolve the temporal or ecological limitations of the design.
